## Concept 1 — ODDLIGHT ★ Recommended

**Tagline:** *The world is stranger than you were told.*

- **Positioning:** true, verified stories about the strangest things in nature, science and space, told like a late-night documentary. Wonder backed by evidence.
- **Audience:** 18–40, curious, international English speakers; people who watch documentaries and science channels and save "I didn't know that" content.
- **Personality:** calm, intimate, precise, quietly amazed. Never shouty, never clickbait.
- **Visual identity:** dark, cinematic frames where **one warm light source** is always present (a lamp in the deep, a sunrise sliver, a bioluminescent glow). This "odd light" is the recurring signature in every opening shot. The look is shallow depth of field, slow camera, fine film grain, and anamorphic-style horizontal flares.
- **Palette:**
  - Ink `#0B0D10` (background)
  - Bone `#EDE6D6` (text)
  - **Sodium `#FF8A1F`** (signature accent: the light)
  - Abyss teal `#1F4E57` (secondary)
  - Signal `#7FD1C7` (data highlights)
- **Typography:** *Fraunces* (soft serif, display and titles), *Inter* (captions and UI). Both are under the SIL Open Font License.
- **Logo direction:** lowercase wordmark "oddlight". The dot of the *i* is replaced by a small glowing sodium-orange circle. The glowing dot alone works as the avatar.
- **Narration:** a warm, low, unhurried voice at about 150 wpm, with short sentences and deliberate pauses before reveals. It is an original designed voice, not a clone.
- **Sound identity:** a 1.5-second signature: a low sub-drone plus a single soft "glass bell" note as the light appears. Ambient textures under the narration, never stock "epic" trailer music.
- **Content categories:** nature anomalies, deep time, space, unsolved and "solved" mysteries, thought experiments.
- **Recurring series:**
  - *Small Impossibles* (animals)
  - *Deep Field* (space)
  - *Unsolved* (evidence-graded mysteries)
  - *Thought Experiment* (what-ifs)
  - *Still Here* (ancient living things)
- **Monetisation:** platform creator funds (YouTube Partner, TikTok Creator Rewards on 1-minute-plus cuts), long-form compilations on YouTube, licensing clips, sponsor segments (science and education apps), prints and posters of the "odd light" stills, and later a newsletter.
- **Why it wins:** it covers 5 of your 7 topic areas under one coherent look, so we can test content directions *without* changing brand. The signature light gives instant recognition in a feed. Factual grounding builds trust and long-term shareability.

## Concept 2 — THE QUIET ARCHIVE

**Tagline:** *Files the world forgot to close.*

- **Positioning:** documentary-style case files on real mysteries, lost places and historical anomalies.
- **Audience:** 20–45, mystery and true-history fans, podcast listeners.
- **Personality:** archivist: measured, sceptical, respectful of evidence.
- **Visual identity:** each video is a numbered "file" ("FILE 014"). Paper, ink and 16 mm textures, archival-looking reconstructions (clearly labelled), and slow push-ins on objects.
- **Palette:** Archive paper `#E9E1CF`, Graphite `#1C1C1C`, Oxblood `#7A1E1E`, Faded cyan `#5B8A8F`.
- **Typography:** IBM Plex Serif + IBM Plex Mono (case numbers, dates).
- **Logo direction:** a monogram stamp "QA" inside a rectangular archive stamp.
- **Narration:** a low, deliberate, slightly British-neutral voice that uses evidence labels ("Confirmed." "Disputed." "Unknown.").
- **Sound identity:** a typewriter carriage plus a paper slide as the opener; a low string drone underneath.
- **Series:** *Open Files*, *Closed Files* (solved mysteries), *Lost Places*, *The Last Record*.
- **Monetisation:** long-form YouTube, a podcast spin-off, sponsorships, a Patreon "full file" tier.
- **Risks:** a narrower topic pool. History reconstructions risk "realistic depictions of real people", so they need strict labelling. The aesthetic is common in the genre.

## Concept 3 — PARALLAX

**Tagline:** *Same universe. Different angle.*

- **Positioning:** hypotheticals and future-tech thought experiments ("What if…", "What happens when…"), with physics and engineering done properly.
- **Audience:** 16–35, tech and science enthusiasts, gamers, sci-fi fans.
- **Personality:** sharp, playful, confident, a bit witty.
- **Visual identity:** clean sci-fi: blueprint overlays, HUD-like measurement graphics (Remotion), cool lighting, scale comparisons.
- **Palette:** Deep navy `#0A1224`, Electric cyan `#2DE2FF`, White `#F5F7FA`, Warning amber `#FFB020`.
- **Typography:** *Space Grotesk* + *JetBrains Mono* (OFL).
- **Logo direction:** two offset chevrons forming a "P" (the parallax shift).
- **Narration:** energetic, crisp, mid-Atlantic.
- **Sound identity:** a synth "shift" whoosh plus a digital tick.
- **Series:** *What If*, *Scale*, *2100*, *Break the Laws* (physics impossibilities).
- **Monetisation:** tech sponsors, education platforms, YouTube long-form.
- **Risks:** "What if" is a crowded genre. Future-tech content dates quickly. Heavier motion-graphics workload per video.

## Recommendation

**ODDLIGHT.** It is the most visually distinctive in a feed, the most scalable in topics, cheap to produce well (dark, slow, atmospheric shots are where today's AI video models make the fewest errors), and it lets us run all three content directions (see 04) under one identity.
